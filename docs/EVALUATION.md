# Evaluation

_Generated by `scripts/build_eval_docs.py` from `docs/eval/*.json`._

Four evaluations on public or hand-checked data, with failure cases. The system under test is the VISTA pipeline as deployed: adaptive sampling at about 2 fps, Kalman/IoU tracking with appearance re-ID, persistent memory, then query, retrieval and a grounded answer, optionally with the verify agent.

## 1. Everyday objects: Objectron, "Where is the X?"

20 phone videos (4 each of cup, bottle, book, laptop, chair) from the Objectron test split, chosen by a fixed random seed. Ground truth is the axis-aligned hull of Objectron's projected 3D box (looser than the visible object). The question goes through the normal parser, retriever and answerer.

- **lead answer**: the first sighting the answer states overlaps the ground truth (IoU >= 0.3). This is the strict, answer-level metric.
- **any-hit**: some returned object overlaps the ground truth. Lenient.
- **claim precision**: share of all returned objects that are correct.

| Run | Found | any-hit | **lead answer** | Objects / query | Claim precision | Frame recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| YOLOv8n, before the fix (laptop) | 85% | 80% | **55%** | 3.5 | 26% | 0.60 |
| YOLOv8n, after the fix (laptop) | 95% | 85% | **75%** | 4.0 | 25% | 0.63 |
| YOLOv8n, before the fix (COOL box, reference) | 90% | 75% | **55%** | 3.5 | 24% | 0.60 |
| OWLv2, before the fix (laptop GPU) | 95% | 90% | **90%** | 16.6 | 6% | 0.86 |
| OWLv2, after the fix (laptop GPU) | 95% | 90% | **90%** | 16.4 | 6% | 0.86 |

Lead-answer hit per class (laptop runs, same clips):

| Class | YOLO before | YOLO after | OWLv2 before | OWLv2 after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| cup | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| bottle | 2/4 | 4/4 | 3/4 | 3/4 |
| book | 1/4 | 2/4 | 3/4 | 3/4 |
| laptop | 3/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| chair | 1/4 | 1/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |

**What changed.** Two changes went in between the runs: the tracker now re-identifies a new detection against occluded tracks (not only lost ones), and "where is X?" answers lead with the most recent, best-supported sighting. On the same machine and clips the YOLO lead answer rose from 55% to 75% and found-rate from 85% to 95%. The OWLv2 numbers did not move, which is why the next paragraph matters.

**Open-vocabulary clutter, diagnosed.** OWLv2 returns many objects per query (up to 132). I first assumed one object was fragmenting into many tracks; the data says otherwise. On the worst clip (`cup/batch-49/23`) OWLv2 reports about 35 "mug" boxes per sampled frame over 41 frames and the tracker keeps 132 of them as objects, but only 4 overlap the real cup. The real cup has the highest support (41 observations at 0.65 confidence vs 0.33-0.52 for the clutter), so ranking leads with it. The remaining problem is persistent false-positive tracks (a patterned tabletop at a 0.2 confidence threshold), which the agent's crop check targets.

Failure cases (figures in `docs/eval/objectron_figs/`, green = ground truth, red = returned object):

- `book/batch-30/0`: a book lying flat on a shop shelf is missed; a hanging packaged flashlight is reported as a notebook.
- `book/batch-7/8` and `chair/batch-25/15`: not found by YOLOv8n (COCO classes handle flat or atypical views poorly).
- `cup/batch-49/23` (OWLv2): 132 returned objects for a single cup, one correct.

## 2. Tracking and memory: MOT17

Four MOT17 training sequences (FRCNN detections are not used; VISTA runs its own detector): 09 (static, easier), 02 and 04 (static, crowded, 1080p), 05 (moving camera, 640x480). Scored on the frames the pipeline sampled, with CLEAR-MOT and IDF1 (own implementation, unit-tested on hand-computed cases), IoU >= 0.5, distractor regions ignored. YOLOv8n on the COOL box. **The 2 fps table is the final tracker (re-run on the COOL instance after the re-link change); the 10 fps table was run before that change.**

**Sampling 2 fps**

| Sequence | MOTA | IDF1 | ID switches | Precision | Recall | Mostly tracked / lost | "Where is the person" grounded | Throughput |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MOT17-09-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.528 | 0.539 | 64 | 0.88 | 0.68 | 0.38 / 0.15 | 0.61 | 29.43 fps |
| MOT17-02-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.151 | 0.221 | 54 | 0.82 | 0.23 | 0.12 / 0.64 | 0.65 | 44.36 fps |
| MOT17-04-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.359 | 0.477 | 41 | 0.90 | 0.42 | 0.13 / 0.47 | 0.70 | 48.15 fps |
| MOT17-05-FRCNN (640x480) | 0.436 | 0.439 | 136 | 0.81 | 0.63 | 0.28 / 0.18 | 0.39 | 19.62 fps |

**Sampling 10 fps**

| Sequence | MOTA | IDF1 | ID switches | Precision | Recall | Mostly tracked / lost | "Where is the person" grounded | Throughput |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MOT17-09-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.577 | 0.527 | 50 | 0.89 | 0.68 | 0.31 / 0.12 | 0.47 | 10.82 fps |
| MOT17-02-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.169 | 0.239 | 43 | 0.81 | 0.23 | 0.14 / 0.64 | 0.47 | 17.04 fps |
| MOT17-04-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.370 | 0.479 | 69 | 0.90 | 0.42 | 0.13 / 0.47 | 0.68 | 9.98 fps |
| MOT17-05-FRCNN (640x480) | 0.463 | 0.487 | 141 | 0.81 | 0.63 | 0.29 / 0.20 | 0.25 | 7.83 fps |

**Reading this honestly.** Precision is high (0.8-0.9) but recall is low on crowded 1080p scenes (0.23 on MOT17-02): the likely cause is that a nano-size YOLO at 640 px input misses small pedestrians (not isolated by a separate experiment), which caps MOTA and IDF1. ID switches are highest with a moving camera (MOT17-05). Sampling at 10 fps instead of 2 fps gives small MOTA gains (+0.01 to +0.05) on all four sequences but roughly triples processing time and does not consistently reduce ID switches, so association, not only sampling, is limiting. These figures are for a lightweight memory pipeline with a small detector, not a state-of-the-art tracker. The re-link change made almost no difference here (MOTA moved by at most 0.003; ID switches on MOT17-05 went from 145 to 136), so it was not the main limit on MOT17.

## 4. Agentic Vision: the verify loop

The agent (`backend/app/agent`, diagram in `docs/agent_workflow.png`) checks the lead sighting with OpenCV before answering: it cuts a square window around the claimed box from the source frame, upscales it and re-runs the detector on the crop. A verified sighting is the answer; a rejected one makes it inspect the next candidate; if nothing verifies it re-scans at denser sampling, and if that still fails it asks the human to choose. Detector output therefore decides the next tool call or the request for human approval. Same Objectron task and detector (`YOLO ONNX (yolov8n.onnx) +labels`) on two sets of 20 clips: the **dev** clips (seed 0) that I used while building and debugging the agent, and **held-out** clips (seed 1) that I only ran once the design was fixed.

| | Dev clips (seed 0) | Held-out clips (seed 1) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Baseline lead answer (no checking) | 75% | 80% |
| Auto-verified answers | 12 / 20 | 17 / 20 |
| Precision of auto-verified answers | 100% | 88% |
| Deferred to the human | 7 | 1 |
| Reported not found | 1 | 2 |
| Correct without the human (of all clips) | 60% | 75% |
| Correct when the human picks among the options shown | 90% | 80% |
| Mean tool calls per question | 5.0 | 2.45 |
| Mean re-scans per question | 0.5 | 0.05 |
| Mean extra time per question | 13.43 s | 1.19 s |

**Reading this honestly.**

- The agent does **not** raise the share of correct answers over the plain lead answer: dev 75% to 60% automatic (90% with the human), held-out 80% to 75% automatic (80% with the human). It trades some automatic answers for checking: when it commits, it is right 100% (dev) and 88% (held-out) of the time, and when it cannot verify it says so or asks.
- The dev clips were used to find a real defect: the first version cut a crop that kept the box's aspect ratio, so thin objects (bottles) were stretched by the detector's square input and 3 of 4 bottle clips were rejected. That version auto-answered 50% of the dev clips and deferred 9 (`docs/eval/agent_yolo_local.json`); the square crop fixed it. The verification threshold (0.35) was not tuned. Because I iterated on the dev clips, the held-out column is the one to trust.
- Held-out sample size is 20: two wrong auto-answers is 12 points of precision. Treat the figures as indicative.
- Against the competition's criterion: vision output changes a later tool call (rescan, next candidate) or triggers a request for human approval, the trace is returned and shown, and failure handling is the ask-the-human step. The loop is deterministic and uses no language model.

Per-clip traces (tool, observation, decision) are in `docs/eval/agent_yolo_local_dev.json` and `docs/eval/agent_yolo_local_heldout.json`; the UI shows the same trace under each answer.

## Re-run on the COOL instance (final commit)

The final commit was deployed to the Graviton5 COOL instance and Objectron and the verify agent were re-run there (`docs/eval/objectron_cool_v2_2fps.json`, `docs/eval/agent_cool_heldout.json`). Lead answer correct 60% (found 90%) with YOLOv8n, against 75% (found 95%) for the same detector on the laptop: the same code and detector gave a different result on the deployed machine (a gap of 3 of 20 clips). I did not isolate the cause; the machines differ in OpenCV build (5.1.0-dev on COOL, pip on the laptop), CPU versus laptop execution and numerics, and I did not test which matters. Treat the laptop figure as the detector's accuracy and this one as what the deployed system delivered. The agent on the held-out clips: 16 verified at 94% precision, 2 deferred, 2 not found; automatic 75%, with the human 80%, plain answer 80%. This is the same conclusion as the laptop run: it does not raise accuracy, it makes answers checkable.

## Limitations

- MOT17 covers pedestrians only (its 10 fps table predates the tracker re-link change); everyday-object evidence comes from 20 Objectron clips, a small sample (per-class n = 4, so one clip moves a class by 25 points).
- Objectron ground truth annotates only the target object, so precision for other objects cannot be measured.
- The hand-checked set is tiny and labelled by one person; it illustrates behaviour, it does not measure it.
- OWLv2 and the before/after comparisons ran on a laptop (GPU for OWLv2), not on the AWS COOL instance; OWLv2 needs PyTorch and is not part of the COOL claim.
- Cross-architecture runs differ on a few low-confidence detections (see BENCHMARK.md).
- The verify agent re-uses the same detector for its second opinion, so it filters unstable detections but cannot catch a consistent mistake such as a printed photo of a face detected as a person; the human step exists for that.

## Responsible use

VISTA stores video, frames and per-person tracks. A printed photo is reported as a person, so person counts and 'who' answers need human review. Video should only be processed with the consent of the people filmed; uploads can be purged by deleting the session prefix in S3 (see `docs/AWS_DEPLOYMENT.md`).

## Data and licences

MOT17 (MOTChallenge) and Objectron (Ahmadyan et al., CVPR 2021) are downloaded at run time and are not in the repository; only four annotated Objectron thumbnails are included, with attribution. Check each dataset's licence before redistributing anything further.
