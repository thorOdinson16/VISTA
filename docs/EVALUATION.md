# Evaluation

_Generated by `scripts/build_eval_docs.py` from `docs/eval/*.json`._

Three evaluations, each on public or hand-checked data, with failure cases. The system under test is the VISTA pipeline as deployed: adaptive sampling at about 2 fps, Kalman/IoU tracking with appearance re-ID, persistent memory, then query, retrieval and a grounded answer.

## 1. Everyday objects: Objectron, "Where is the X?"

20 phone videos (4 each of cup, bottle, book, laptop, chair) from the Objectron test split, chosen by a fixed random seed. Ground truth is the axis-aligned hull of Objectron's projected 3D box (looser than the visible object). The question goes through the normal parser, retriever and answerer. Metrics:

- **any-hit**: some returned object's last sighting overlaps the ground truth (IoU >= 0.3). Lenient.
- **latest-sighting hit**: the most recent sighting among returned objects overlaps the ground truth. This is the strict answer-level metric ("where was it last seen").
- **claim precision**: share of all returned objects whose last sighting is correct.

| Detector | Found | any-hit | latest-sighting hit | Claim precision | Objects returned / query | Frame recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| YOLOv8n via `cv2.dnn` (COOL box) | 90% | 75% | 60% | 24% | 3.5 | 0.60 |
| OWLv2 (open vocabulary, local GPU) | 95% | 90% | 90% | 6% | 16.6 | 0.86 |

Latest-sighting hit per class:

| Class | YOLOv8n | OWLv2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| cup | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| bottle | 4/4 | 3/4 |
| book | 1/4 | 3/4 |
| laptop | 3/4 | 4/4 |
| chair | 0/4 | 4/4 |

**Reading this honestly.** The open-vocabulary detector fixes the classes a closed-set COCO model handles badly (chair, flat books). But both detectors return many fragmented objects per query (OWLv2 up to 135), so only a small share of returned objects are correct. The most likely cause is that a handheld camera orbiting an object makes the IoU tracker lose and re-create tracks (not isolated by a separate experiment). The answer is right when the system leads with the latest sighting, wrong if it simply lists everything it remembers. Consolidating track fragments is the main identified weakness.

Failure cases (figures in `docs/eval/objectron_figs/`, green = ground truth, red = returned object):

- `book/batch-30/0`: a book lying flat on a shop shelf is missed; a hanging packaged flashlight is reported as a notebook.
- `book/batch-7/8` and `chair/batch-25/15`: not found by YOLOv8n (COCO classes handle thin/flat or atypical views poorly).
- `cup/batch-49/23` (OWLv2): 135 returned objects for a single cup; `bottle/batch-33/34`: 75 for a single bottle.

## 2. Tracking and memory: MOT17

Four MOT17 training sequences (FRCNN detections are not used; VISTA runs its own detector): 09 (static, easier), 02 and 04 (static, crowded, 1080p), 05 (moving camera, 640x480). Scored on the frames the pipeline sampled, with CLEAR-MOT and IDF1 (own implementation, unit-tested on hand-computed cases), IoU >= 0.5, distractor regions ignored. YOLOv8n on the COOL box.

**Sampling 2 fps**

| Sequence | MOTA | IDF1 | ID switches | Precision | Recall | Mostly tracked / lost | "Where is the person" grounded | Throughput |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MOT17-09-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.528 | 0.539 | 64 | 0.88 | 0.68 | 0.38 / 0.15 | 0.59 | 29.4 fps |
| MOT17-02-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.151 | 0.221 | 54 | 0.82 | 0.23 | 0.12 / 0.64 | 0.65 | 44.3 fps |
| MOT17-04-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.359 | 0.478 | 42 | 0.90 | 0.42 | 0.13 / 0.47 | 0.71 | 48.08 fps |
| MOT17-05-FRCNN (640x480) | 0.433 | 0.441 | 145 | 0.81 | 0.63 | 0.28 / 0.18 | 0.35 | 19.48 fps |

**Sampling 10 fps**

| Sequence | MOTA | IDF1 | ID switches | Precision | Recall | Mostly tracked / lost | "Where is the person" grounded | Throughput |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MOT17-09-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.577 | 0.527 | 50 | 0.89 | 0.68 | 0.31 / 0.12 | 0.47 | 10.82 fps |
| MOT17-02-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.169 | 0.239 | 43 | 0.81 | 0.23 | 0.14 / 0.64 | 0.47 | 17.04 fps |
| MOT17-04-FRCNN (1920x1080) | 0.370 | 0.479 | 69 | 0.90 | 0.42 | 0.13 / 0.47 | 0.68 | 9.98 fps |
| MOT17-05-FRCNN (640x480) | 0.463 | 0.487 | 141 | 0.81 | 0.63 | 0.29 / 0.20 | 0.25 | 7.83 fps |

**Reading this honestly.** Precision is high (0.8-0.9) but recall is low on crowded 1080p scenes (0.23 on MOT17-02): the likely cause is that a nano-size YOLO at 640 px input misses small pedestrians (not isolated by a separate experiment), which caps MOTA and IDF1. ID switches are highest with a moving camera (MOT17-05). Sampling at 10 fps instead of 2 fps gives small MOTA gains (+0.01 to +0.05) on all four sequences but roughly triples processing time and does not consistently reduce ID switches, so association, not only sampling, is limiting. These figures are for a lightweight memory pipeline with a small detector, not a state-of-the-art tracker.

## 3. Hand-checked clips (small, qualitative)

Five questions on two short clips (a desk scene; a phone charger on a bedsheet), labelled by viewing frames (`docs/eval/clips_spec.json`). Run through the real API, including the on-demand open-vocabulary re-scan. The clips are the author's own and are not distributed.

| Question | Expected | YOLOv8n | OWLv2 + on-demand re-scan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where is the laptop? | found | PASS (last seen 4.49 s) | PASS (last seen 4.49 s) |
| Where is the power adapter? | found | FAIL (not found) | PASS (last seen 4.24 s) |
| Where is the person? | not_found | FAIL (last seen 3.74 s) | FAIL (last seen 3.99 s) |
| Where is the phone charger? | found | FAIL (not found) | PASS (last seen 3.52 s) |
| Where is the cat? | not_found | PASS (not found) | PASS (not found) |

Score: YOLOv8n 2/5, OWLv2 4/5. Both detectors report a **printed photograph of a face as a person**; the closed-set model additionally cannot be asked about objects outside its 80 classes (power adapter, phone charger) while the on-demand open-vocabulary re-scan can.

## Limitations

- MOT17 covers pedestrians only; everyday-object evidence comes from 20 Objectron clips, which is a small sample (per-class n = 4, so single clips move class numbers by 25 points).
- Objectron ground truth annotates only the target object, so precision for other objects cannot be measured.
- The hand-checked set is tiny and labelled by one person; it illustrates behaviour, it does not measure it.
- OWLv2 results were produced on a local GPU, not on the AWS COOL instance (it needs PyTorch and is not part of the COOL claim).
- Cross-architecture runs differ on a few low-confidence detections (see BENCHMARK.md).

## Responsible use

VISTA stores video, frames and per-person tracks. A printed photo is reported as a person, so person counts and 'who' answers need human review. Video should only be processed with the consent of the people filmed; uploads can be purged by deleting the session prefix in S3 (see `docs/AWS_DEPLOYMENT.md`).

## Data and licences

MOT17 (MOTChallenge) and Objectron (Ahmadyan et al., CVPR 2021) are downloaded at run time and are not in the repository; only four annotated Objectron thumbnails are included, with attribution. Check each dataset's licence before redistributing anything further.
