# COOL / Graviton benchmark

_Generated by `scripts/build_eval_docs.py` from `docs/eval/bench_*.json`._

## What was measured

The same OpenCV workload (`scripts/bench_cv.py`) on three instances, using one 1440x1920 phone clip (Objectron `cup/batch-30/2`, 120 frames for per-frame stages). Each stage is warmed up once and timed 5 times; the table shows the median. The **pipeline** row is the full VISTA pipeline (decode, adaptive sampling, optical flow, MOG2, `cv2.dnn` YOLO, tracking, memory) on the whole 269-frame clip.

## What the numbers say

- **Graviton beats x86 on this workload.** The Graviton5 instance runs the full pipeline 1.37x (COOL) / 1.33x (pip) as fast as the m8i x86 instance and is cheaper per hour, so cost per million frames is $2.79 (pip Arm) vs $4.02 (x86), 31% lower. Per stage, COOL is faster than x86 on 6 of 7 stages and pip Arm on 5 of 7; x86 is ahead on `dnn_yolo_640` (COOL); `orb_features`, `dnn_yolo_640` (pip Arm).
- **COOL versus stock pip OpenCV on the same Arm hardware is within noise for the full pipeline:** 20.06 vs 19.51 fps (+2.8%). COOL wins clearly on ORB feature extraction (1.44x) and is marginally ahead on optical flow, but is slower on decode, resize and `cv2.dnn` inference. Because COOL carries a $0.01/h fee, cost per million frames is $2.85 vs $2.79 for pip.
- **The pip arm64 wheel already ships KleidiCV** (version 26.03 in the build string above, newer than COOL's 0.7.0), so stock OpenCV on Graviton is already Arm-optimised; COOL's advantage over it is much smaller than over an unoptimised baseline.
- **Detection accuracy is unchanged by the build.** COOL and pip-Arm produced identical outcomes on all 20 Objectron clips; the x86 run differed on 2 of 20 low-confidence book clips (floating-point differences in `cv2.dnn` output across architectures).

## Caveats

- COOL is OpenCV 5.1.0-dev; the newest pip release is 5.0.0. The comparison is COOL against what a user gets from pip, not a controlled isolate of the Arm optimisations.
- One clip, one instance per configuration, 5 repeats each: no variance across instances or days. Differences of a few percent should be treated as noise.
- Vendor-published figures (up to 1.68x on image processing) were not reproduced on this workload; the workload here is the real VISTA pipeline, which is dominated by decode, `cv2.dnn` and tracking.
